**Particle-Based Computer Simulation of Colloidal Systems** is a computational physics research project investigating the behavior of colloidal particles constrained to a circular geometry and interacting through a competing Lennard-Jones potential. The study uses molecular simulations to analyze particle dynamics and characterize how competing attractive and repulsive interactions influence the structural and dynamical properties of the system.

### Key Highlights
- **Molecular Dynamics Simulations:** Simulated colloidal particle systems constrained to a circular geometry using computational methods.
- **Particle Interaction Modeling:** Implemented simulations involving competing attractive and repulsive interactions through a Lennard-Jones-based potential.
- **Stochastic Particle Dynamics:** Applied the Ermak–McCammon algorithm to model particle motion under the influence of interparticle forces and diffusion.
- **Statistical Analysis:** Calculated angular distributions, mean-square angular displacement, angular density, and the Van Hove correlation function.
- **Scientific Collaboration:** Co-authored a computational physics research study with researchers from the Universidad de Guanajuato.

### Tech Stack & Methodology
- **Scientific Computing (`Fortran`):** Implementing numerical algorithms and computational simulations to study interacting particle systems.
- **Linux Environment (`Linux + Bash`):** Running simulation workflows and managing computational experiments through command-line tools.
- **Data Analysis:** Processing simulation results to characterize particle distributions and dynamical behavior.
- **Scientific Documentation (`LaTeX`):** Preparing a technical research paper documenting the physical model, numerical methods, and results.
